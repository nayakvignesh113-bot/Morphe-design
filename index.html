What the numbers mean.
  • 2D plane-stress, linear-elastic, static analysis of a plate of uniform thickness. Stress is element-centroid von Mises; elements touching a point load are excluded from the peak because a point load has no finite stress. Your fastener detail sets that local stress.
  • Several load cases (separate situations the part must survive, not loads applied together): each design minimizes the summed compliance of all cases, and is then checked against the worst case for stress, deflection and buckling.
  • Mesh check: after each run, every design's cut outline is re-analysed on meshes 2× (and, when affordable, 3×) finer. Sizing uses the finest mesh, and the report shows how much the numbers moved. Peak stress at rounded junctions converges from below (the optimizer mesh under-reads it by 10–25%), so it is extrapolated from the two finest meshes (first-order Richardson) and sizing uses that value; a peak still rising more than 10% on the last refinement is rejected as a sharp corner. Stress within three optimizer elements of a support or point load is reported separately as a hot spot, because it depends on the joint detail rather than the shape.
  • Thickness is solved exactly: stress scales with 1/t, deflection with 1/(E·t) and the buckling load with E·t³. The largest requirement wins, then it is rounded up to the next standard plate gauge.
  • Buckling is a linear eigenvalue check of out-of-plane plate buckling. Bolted supports are clamped, sliding supports are simply supported, and load points are free out of plane. A factor of 3 or more allows for imperfections; real parts with large initial bow need more.
  • Not checked: out-of-plane applied loads, fatigue, contact and bearing at bolt holes. Before release, rerun the exported geometry in your FEA package.
  • Your setup is kept in this browser between visits. Use Save project to keep or share it as a file.
  • Solver verified against the published 88-line topology code (identical compliance history), Timoshenko beam theory (tip deflection within 0.1%), simply supported plate buckling k = 4 (within 0.3%) and the Euler cantilever column (within 0.4%).
