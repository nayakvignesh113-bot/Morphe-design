What do the tools mean?
Support
Where the part is held. Fixed (bolted) holds it in both directions. Slides horizontally holds it only vertically, like a roller, and slides vertically holds it only horizontally. Drag along an edge for a clamped face, tap for a single bolt point.
Load
A force on the part, any size and direction: set them in the toolbar before tapping, drag to aim, or edit Fx and Fy afterwards (N; +x right, +y up).
Load case
A separate situation the part must survive (working load, a knock, a reversed load). Loads in the same case act together; every design is checked against the worst case.
Bolt hole
A real hole for a standard bolt (M4 to M20, ISO clearance size) with a ring of material 1.5 d around it. A support bolt holds the hole edge; a load pin pushes on the side of the hole facing the load. The tool checks plate bearing, bolt shear (class 8.8) and edge distance, and exports the hole as an exact circle.
Keep-out
A region that must stay empty: clearance for a bolt head, shaft, cable or neighbouring part. The optimizer never puts material there. Give rectangles a corner radius, because square inner corners concentrate stress.
Keep-solid
A region that must stay material: a mounting flange, a face that another part sits on, the area around a hole you will drill. The optimizer always keeps it.

What the numbers mean.
  • 2D plane-stress, linear-elastic, static analysis of a plate of uniform thickness. Stress is element-centroid von Mises; elements touching a point load are excluded from the peak because a point load has no finite stress. Your fastener detail sets that local stress.
  • Several load cases (separate situations the part must survive, not loads applied together): each design minimizes the summed compliance of all cases, and is then checked against the worst case for stress, deflection and buckling.
  • Mesh check: after each run, every design's cut outline is re-analysed on meshes 2× (and, when affordable, 3×) finer. Sizing uses the finest mesh, and the report shows how much the numbers moved. Peak stress at rounded junctions converges from below (the optimizer mesh under-reads it by 10–25%), so it is extrapolated from the two finest meshes (first-order Richardson), a 10% discretisation allowance is applied (calibrated against an independent solver, see benchmark/), and sizing uses the larger value; a peak still rising more than 10% on the last refinement is rejected as a sharp corner. Stress within three optimizer elements of a support or point load is reported separately as a hot spot, because it depends on the joint detail rather than the shape.
  • Bolt holes: support bolts hold the hole edge (bonded, slightly stiffer than a real clearance fit); a load pin applies cosine pressure on the loaded half of the hole. Plate bearing σ = F/(d·t) is checked against yield and enters thickness sizing; bolt shear uses class 8.8 through the threads (0.577 Sy on the stress area). Inside the material ring of a bolted or pinned hole, the bearing check governs and that ring is left out of the FE peak; free holes count in full.
  • Thickness is solved exactly: stress scales with 1/t, deflection with 1/(E·t) and the buckling load with E·t³. The largest requirement wins, then it is rounded up to the next standard plate gauge.
  • Buckling is a linear eigenvalue check of out-of-plane plate buckling. Bolted supports are clamped, sliding supports are simply supported, and load points are free out of plane. A factor of 3 or more allows for imperfections; real parts with large initial bow need more.
  • Fatigue (when loading is repeated or reversed): modified Goodman on the mesh-checked peak von Mises stress, against a Marin-corrected strength (surface from the process, axial loading 0.85, 99% reliability 0.814). Steel and titanium use the endurance limit; aluminium has none, so its strength at 5×10⁸ cycles is used. Each load case is treated as cycling on its own. Plastics have no reliable data and are not checked. Fretting at bolted joints and welds are outside this check.
  • Not checked: out-of-plane applied loads, bolt preload and friction, fretting, welds. Before release, rerun the exported geometry in your FEA package.

The numbers here assume rounding of about one finest-mesh element (0.7–0.9 mm); a sharper corner reads higher (in the benchmark, one design's peak rose 14% when the mesh was halved). Specify an inner corner radius of at least 1 mm when you clean the outline in CAD.
